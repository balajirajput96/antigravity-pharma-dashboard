# Reel 0003 Source Metadata — Retrieval Practice

## Scope

This record accompanies the locally QA-passed Hindi reel, **“बार-बार पढ़ना नहीं—याद से निकालना”**. It supports a limited educational claim about retrieval practice. It is not medical, therapeutic, diagnostic, or score-guarantee advice.

## Evidence Classification

| Claim in reel | Classification | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Practice tests/retrieval practice can outperform rereading in many comparisons. | Research synthesis | Effects vary by task, population, implementation, feedback, and timing. |
| Closing notes and trying to recall key points is a practical learning exercise. | Evidence-aligned suggestion | It is not a promise of improved grades or a universal outcome. |

2. **Agarwal, P. K., Nunes, L. D., & Blunt, J. R. (2021).** Retrieval practice consistently benefits student learning: a systematic review of applied research in schools and classrooms. *Educational Psychology Review, 33*, 1409–1453. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9
3. **Trumble, B. C., et al. (2023).** Retrieval practice in health professions education: a systematic review. *Medical Education*, 57(6), 515–529. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.15049

## Source-Use Notes

The Adesope meta-analysis is used for the general comparative claim. The Agarwal review is used to support application in varied classroom implementations, not to promise a particular result. The Trumble review is used only to retain the implementation-heterogeneity boundary. The reel does not state effect sizes, population-specific figures, or causal guarantees.
