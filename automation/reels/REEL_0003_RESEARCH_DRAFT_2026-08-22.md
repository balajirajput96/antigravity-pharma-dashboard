# Reel 0003 — Retrieval Practice Research Draft

**Status:** Discovery only; not a source-verification record and not a production authorization.

**Proposed angle:** Recalling information from memory (for example, answering a question without looking) can support learning more than passive rereading in many studied settings. The Reel must not claim a guarantee, a medical benefit, a fixed score increase, or universal superiority in every context.

## Independently Extracted Findings and Limits

| Evidence record | Findings usable in a cautious script | Scope and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trumble et al. (2023) [1] | This systematic review searched seven databases, included 56 eligible records/63 experiments in health-professions education, and reported that 43 experiments found significant benefits of distributed practice and/or retrieval practice over comparison groups. | This is an education-specific review; study designs and interventions were heterogeneous, so the Reel must not imply one universal effect size or method. |
| Agarwal, Nunes & Blunt (2021) [2] | The classroom-focused systematic review screened nearly 2,000 abstracts and coded 50 experiments; it reports 49 effect sizes and a total sample of 5,374, with 57% of effect sizes showing medium or large benefits. | The review reports variable implementations and notes that only 6% of experiments occurred in non-WEIRD countries. The Reel must frame the approach as evidence-supported across many studied contexts, not guaranteed for every learner or setting. |
| Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan (2017) [3] | The meta-analysis abstract reports that practice tests were more beneficial for learning than restudying and other comparison conditions in the studies analysed. | The abstract also states that effects were moderated by test features, participant/study characteristics, outcome constructs, and methodology. The Reel must name context-dependence and avoid a one-size-fits-all claim. |

## Claim-Control Rules

Scientific evidence, a learner’s strategy choice, and any philosophy of education must remain distinct. Only claims directly supported by the independently extracted sources may appear in the script. “Recall, then check” is a possible practical illustration, not a prescription or personal-performance guarantee.
