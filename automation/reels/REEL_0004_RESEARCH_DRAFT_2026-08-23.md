# Reel 0004 Research Draft — Spacing Is Not a Magic Calendar

## Candidate Angle

**Topic:** Learning and memory

**Unique angle:** The spacing effect supports distributing repeated learning episodes over time, but research does not justify a fixed “every 24 hours” calendar, a universal interval, or an automatic memory guarantee.

**State:** Evidence-verified research draft only. No Hindi script, narration, visual asset, final media, QA package, Drive package, or completion count is created by this record.

## Source Review

| Source | What it supports | Boundary for use in Reel 0004 |
|---|---|---|
| Cepeda et al. (2006), *Psychological Bulletin*, quantitative review, DOI: [10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354), PubMed: [PMID 16719566](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/) | The review examined 839 distributed-practice assessments across 317 experiments in 184 articles. The authors reported that the interstudy interval and retention interval jointly affected final-test retention; the interval associated with maximal retention increased as the later retention interval increased. | Do not convert the synthesis into an exact personal schedule or a fixed score claim. The reviewed evidence concerns particular study designs and verbal-recall tasks. |
| Vlach & Sandhofer (2012), *Child Development*, DOI: [10.1111/j.1467-8624.2012.01781.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2012.01781.x), full text: [PMC3399982](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3399982/) | In one study of 36 children aged 5–7 learning science concepts, schedules separated across days produced higher generalization performance than immediate/massed schedules on the study’s task. | Do not generalize this single child sample to every age, subject, learner, or outcome. It is an example of an educationally relevant implementation, not a universal prescription. |
| Latimier, Peyre & Ramus (2021), *Educational Psychology Review*, DOI: [10.1007/s10648-020-09572-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09572-8) | In a meta-analysis of 29 studies on spacing **retrieval-practice** episodes, spaced retrieval showed a benefit over massed retrieval in the authors’ subset. The review did not find a significant overall advantage for expanding rather than uniform intervals. | This evidence mixes spacing with retrieval practice, so use it only to reject simplistic “always lengthen every interval” advice. Do not present it as a new treatment, grade, or memory guarantee. |

## Evidence Classification

| Candidate statement | Classification | Approved wording boundary |
|---|---|---|
| “Repeated study sessions spread across time can support later retention better than immediately massed repetitions in many studied contexts.” | Strong evidence, qualified | Say “can” and name the comparison; do not say it works for everyone. |
| “There is no one magic spacing interval for all goals.” | Strongly supported qualification | Explain that study-test delay and context matter; avoid exact calendar rules. |
| “Make your next review plan deliberately rather than cramming everything together.” | Practical suggestion | Frame as a low-risk learning experiment, not a promised outcome. |
| “Spacing cures forgetfulness / guarantees marks / changes the brain permanently.” | Unsupported or overstated | Exclude. |
