## Independently verified evidence boundary

The source record’s principal scientific distinction is supported by the retrieved publications. A 2025 meta-analysis reports **no overall memory advantage** for unfinished tasks and a general tendency to resume interrupted tasks; it characterizes the classic Zeigarnik memory claim as lacking universal validity.[1] The 2026 work-recovery meta-analysis reports associations between unfinished work tasks and work-related thoughts during off-job time, not a universal causal rule or a diagnosis.[2]

> Production-safe statement: the popular claim that unfinished tasks are *always* remembered better is too broad. The evidence supports a context-dependent resumption tendency, while work-related thoughts are an association observed in workplace research.

Accordingly, Reel 0002 must not use a percentage claim, a literal “brain RAM” neuroscience explanation, a promise to fix rumination/sleep, or a medical or mental-health interpretation. A practical “write the next small step” suggestion can be framed as a planning prompt rather than a treatment or guaranteed outcome.
